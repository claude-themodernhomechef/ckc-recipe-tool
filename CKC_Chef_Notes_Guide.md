# CKC Chef's Notes — Style & Content Guide

## What Chef's Notes Are

Chef's Notes are personal cooking tips from a private chef's kitchen notebook. They tell you how to make a dish better — through technique, flavor upgrades, smarter sourcing, practical shortcuts, and nutritional improvements. They are written as if a highly trained private chef is sharing their prep notes with another cook.

**Chef's Notes are NEVER about diet protocols, ingredient swaps for dietary compliance, or any mention of Gluten-Free, Dairy-Free, Vegan, Vegetarian, Keto, AIP, Low-FODMAP, or Low-Histamine.** Those are handled separately in swap_notes and diet_compliant_notes.

---

## Voice

- **First person plural: "We"** — always "We like to," "We find," "We prefer," "We recommend"
- **Never** "I," "You should," or "Try this"
- **Kitchen notebook tone** — practical, specific, no fluff, no personality beyond "We like to"
- **Include the why** when it adds value — "We season mushrooms after sautéing — salt draws out moisture and creates water in the pan"
- **Spice and acid measurements in ranges** — "1/2–1 tsp smoked paprika," "1–3 tbsp lemon juice," "2–3 tbsp butter"
- **Temperatures in Fahrenheit with doneness cues** — "broil at 475–500 F for 8–12 minutes, until internal temperature reaches 165 F"

## The 7 Categories

Every chef's note should fall into one or more of these categories.

### 1. Nutritional Upgrades (without saying "nutrition")

Sneaking in more vegetables, fiber, and protein without making it sound like a health lecture. Always name at least one specific vegetable, but include "or whatever you have on hand" for flexibility.

**Examples from Rafi's notes:**
- "We like to add 2–3 oz of fresh baby spinach and 3/4 c shredded zucchini to this recipe."
- "We like to add canned cooked lentils that have been drained and rinsed to our cooked rice."
- "We like to add sauteed dark greens to this recipe like kale or baby spinach."
- "We like to do a mix of white rice and cauliflower rice as the base."
- "We like to add roasted broccoli or zucchini, or whatever vegetable you have on hand."
- "We like to add chickpeas to the herb salad for more fiber."

**Bone broth rule:** Recommend chicken bone broth when the recipe uses broth or water as a primary cooking liquid — soups, braises, rice/grain pilafs, liquid-based sauces. Frame it as a recommendation: "We recommend using a high-collagen chicken bone broth here for added protein and a richer body." Skip it for dry-heat cooking (roasts, sheet pans, grills), salads, and dishes that are already protein-dense. **Never recommend bone broth for vegan or vegetarian dishes.**

**Bone broth by dish type (from Rafi's data):**
- Soups: ~85% get the recommendation
- Grain sides (rice, pilaf, couscous): recommend when broth is the cooking liquid
- Saucy entrees (braises, curries, tagines): recommend when liquid is central
- Dry-heat entrees, salads, roasts: skip

### 2. Technique

Specific cooking methods, temperatures, times, and the reasoning behind them. This is where the private chef knowledge shines — things a home cook wouldn't know.

**Examples from Rafi's notes:**
- "We find seasoning the mushrooms after sautéing to be the best method for crisping up mushrooms. Salt draws out the moisture and will create water in the pan."
- "We find adding chicken thigh with skin on to a cold pan and then turning on the heat will result in crispier skin. The fat from the skin renders evenly."
- "We like to broil the chicken on the second rack from the top at 475–500 F for 8–12 minutes (until the internal temperature of the chicken is 165 F)."
- "We process the potatoes using a tamis (fine mesh sieve) to yield the creamiest mashed potatoes."
- "We like to pound these out super thin, almost paper thin to get maximum crust."
- "We like to refrigerate the schnitzel for at least an hour so the breading can adhere to the chicken."
- "Mixing the wet ingredients with the breadcrumbs and seasonings first, and allowing the breadcrumbs to absorb the liquid before incorporating the ground beef, creates a better bind."
- "We find roasting the cauliflower on foil instead of parchment paper results in crispier florets."
- "We find you must boil the sweet potatoes just before they are fork-tender before grilling. We like to grill on high heat until they get the char (3–5 minutes per side)."

### 3. Flavor Depth

Umami boosters, extra spice, blooming aromatics in fat, ingredient upgrades that add complexity. These are the moves that separate a good home cook from a private chef.

**Umami boosters (use when the dish has a sauce or liquid base):**
- Fish sauce (1–2 tsp) — salmon, meatballs, steak, aguachile
- Yellow miso (1 tbsp) — stews, cottage pie, pasta sauces
- Nutritional yeast (2 tbsp) — soups, dressings, pesto
- Porcini mushroom powder (1 tsp) — broths, soups
- Tamari (1–2 tbsp) — braises, pot roasts, marinades
- Parmesan (1/4–1/2 c freshly grated) — soups, sauces, pastas
- Bouillon cubes — rice, pilafs
- Anchovy paste or fish sauce in meatballs — "adds complexity without tasting like fish"

**Spice upgrades (always in ranges):**
- "We like to add 1/2–1 tsp smoked paprika and 1/2–1 tsp ground cumin."
- "We like to add 1/2 tsp of Chinese Five Spice to add more complexity to the sauce."
- "We like to double the spice recipe and bloom it in the olive oil first thing."
- "We like to add 1–2 tsp ground ceylon cinnamon at the same time you toast the cumin seeds."

**Examples from Rafi's notes:**
- "We like to add 2 tbsp of nutritional yeast and 1 tsp of porcini mushroom powder for more umami."
- "We like to add 1 tbsp of yellow miso for extra depth."
- "We like to add 2 tsp ground anchovy paste or 1 tsp of fish sauce to the meatballs if we have some on hand. This adds more complexity."
- "We like to alter the recipe and add 2–3 tbsp butter, and brown it before toasting the spices."
- "We like to replace the extra virgin olive oil with the oil from the sundried tomatoes to sear the chicken."

### 4. Acid and Brightness

Finishing with lemon, lime, vinegar, or zest. Nearly every savory dish benefits from a hit of acid at the end.

**Examples from Rafi's notes:**
- "We like to finish the dish with a fresh squeeze of 1/2 lemon."
- "We like to add the juice of 2 limes to the soup while it's cooking."
- "We like to add the lemon zest as well as extra lemon juice."
- "We like to add 2 tbsp lime juice or rice vinegar to the marinade if you don't have enough time to marinate overnight."
- "We like to add the zest of 1 orange to the base of the dish."

### 5. Sauce Math

More sauce is almost always the right call. Double it, reduce it, make it separately.

**Examples from Rafi's notes:**
- "We like to double the sauce recipe to add more on top of the bowl."
- "We like to 1.5x the sauce recipe to pour over the bowl."
- "We make the sauce in a small saucepan, separate from the pork, and simmer it until thickened."
- "We like to remove the lamb once cooked and add 1/4 balsamic glaze and 1/4 c tamari and reduce until we have a third of the sauce left."
- "We like to reserve 1/2 the marinade and reduce it on the stovetop until it becomes a syrup consistency."

### 6. Sourcing and Protein Cuts

How a private chef selects and portions protein. Specific cuts, how to talk to a butcher/fishmonger, and portion math.

**Portioning rule: ~1/2 lb protein per person for entrees.** Lighter proteins (shrimp, tofu) in bowl/noodle dishes where protein isn't the star can be slightly less, but call it out if the original recipe under-portions.

**Examples from Rafi's notes:**
- "We ask the fishmonger for center-cut filets with the skin removed and deboned."
- "We ask the butcher to remove the fat and silver skin."
- "We only use fresh salmon for this recipe."
- "We prefer bone-in, skinless chicken thighs for this recipe."
- "We like to use hanger steak for this recipe."
- "We like to use a mix of half chicken breast and half thighs."
- "We like to replace short ribs with 3 lb chuck roast cut into 3-inch rectangles."
- "We like to combine 1 lb lean ground beef (90%) with 1/2 lb ground lamb."

### 7. Practical Methods and Shortcuts

Instant Pot conversions, alternative cooking methods, easier prep. A private chef values efficiency.

**Instant Pot pattern (for braises/pot roasts):** Sear deeply on all sides → pressure cook on high for 35 minutes → allow steam to naturally release (20–30 minutes) → reduce sauce.

**Examples from Rafi's notes:**
- "We like to add everything to an instant pot and cook on high for 35 minutes, allowing the steam to naturally release (20 minutes). Shred the pork and reduce the sauce until it becomes sticky."
- "Grilling fish can be tricky. An alternative method is placing the filets on an oiled foil-lined baking tray and broil in the top 1/3 rack for 7–9 minutes."
- "We find cooking it on the stovetop in a cast iron over medium-high heat works just as well."
- "For an easier method we like to make a sheet-pan version where we spread the meat to an even square with 1/2 inch thickness, and cut the kabobs in 2-inch strips."
- "We like to use Rao's marinara to replace tomatoes in the recipe for a quicker meal."

## Additional Patterns

### Lighter Options
We like suggesting lighter versions without making it about dieting:
- "We like to replace heavy cream with light coconut milk."
- "We find reducing the butter in half works just as well."
- "We like to remove 1/4 c of oil and replace with 2 tbsp of water to thin out the dressing."
- "We like to add 1 c chicken bone broth and reduce the amount of cream by half."

### Fresh Herb Flexibility
Name at least one specific herb, but leave room for what's on hand:
- "We like to switch up the fresh herbs with what we have on hand. Cilantro, dill, and basil work well."
- "We like to add a mix of dill, parsley and cilantro."
- "We like to change the herbs to what we have on hand, like adding fresh dill or fresh marjoram."

### "Use What You Have" Philosophy
Always name a specific suggestion first, then open it up:
- "We like to add more vegetables to the stuffing. Shredded zucchini, cauliflower rice, spinach or whatever vegetable you have on hand."
- "Whatever toasted nuts you have will work great with this recipe."
- "We like to serve with extra roasted vegetables we have on hand like zucchini or summer squash rounds, kale, or broccoli."

### Marination
- "We like to marinate at least 12 hours, preferably 24."
- "We like to season the steak at least 30 minutes prior to cooking and allow it to sit at room temperature."

### Cross-Recipe Utilization
When recipes are part of a meal, note how components connect:
- "We will use up the leftover broth from the Salisbury steak for this recipe."
- "We like to add leftover herbs from the meatballs."
- "We find the parsley oil from the couscous recipe ties the dish together."

## Complexity Matching

Match the number of notes to the complexity of the dish:

| Dish type | Notes | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Simple sides, sauces, dressings | 1 note (or none if truly nothing to add) | Steamed rice, guacamole, simple vinaigrette |
| Standard entrees, most sides | 2–3 notes | Cashew chicken, salmon burgers, Greek meatball bowl |
| Multi-component meals, technique-heavy dishes | 4+ notes | Moroccan meatball soup, fish soup, turkey zucchini lasagna |

**The rule:** The more components, technique steps, or cooking methods a dish has, the more notes it earns. A dressing gets 1. A braise with a side and a sauce gets 4.

**When to skip notes entirely:** Only when the dish is so simple that any note would feel forced — plain steamed vegetables with no seasoning opportunity, a recipe that's already perfect as-is. This should be rare.

## What Chef's Notes Are NOT

- **Not diet compliance swaps** — never mention Gluten-Free, Dairy-Free, Vegan, Vegetarian, Keto, AIP, Low-FODMAP, Low-Histamine
- **Not recipe rewrites** — we're suggesting tweaks, not replacing the recipe
- **Not generic advice** — "season to taste" or "use fresh ingredients" adds nothing
- **Not brand recommendations** — keep generic for now (future Instacart integration will handle product specifics)
- **Not nutritional lectures** — we add bone broth "for added protein and a richer body," not "because it's healthier"
