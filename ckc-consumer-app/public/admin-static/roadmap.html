Parser improvements (live)
  • Strips prep words anywhere in the name — chopped, minced, grated, diced, sliced, finely, freshly, squeezed, warmed, toasted, trimmed, etc. "1/4 cup chopped parsley" → "0.25 cup parsley".
  • Strips size descriptor "medium" (default — uninformative). Keeps "small", "large", "big", "jumbo" — they matter at the store ("8 small tortillas" ≠ large).
  • Strips bare recipe-note suffixes — "see notes above", "preferably <X>", "such as <X>", "ideally <X>", "depending on <X>", "plus more for <X>".
  • Skips section headers — "For the dressing:", "Sauce:", etc. don't show up as ingredients.
  • Surfaces serving markers in the qty column — "lime wedges, to squeeze over fajitas" displays as to serve | lime wedges. Markers: to serve, to garnish, to taste.
  • Canonicalizes broth — "vegetable broth/stock" → "vegetable broth"; "chicken stock" / "beef stock" all alias to "broth".
  • "can <X>" → "canned <X>" so the shopper knows it's canned, not dry. Exception: "can ... coconut milk" strips "can" entirely (canned is implicit for coconut milk).
  • Salt+pepper compound widened — "kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper" now collapses to "salt + pepper".
  • Form modifiers kept for shopping — "bone-in skin-on chicken" stays as-is on the shopping list (consumer needs it). The nutrition matcher additionally strips them for DB lookup.
Feedback loop — every text edit teaches the matcher

When you edit an ingredient name in the Review Queue, the correction now also writes to a global ingredientAliases collection. The matcher consults this collection on its next run, so every correction improves all future recipes — not just the one being edited.

Review queue — approve recipes before they go live
Done

After the enrichment pipeline finishes, recipes land here for your final check. You can edit diet tags, fix chef notes, update ingredients, and then hit "Approve → Consumer" to push the recipe live. Approving sets its status to "approved" in Firebase, which is exactly what the consumer app's Discover feed queries for. Rejecting sends it back to "no" and removes it from the queue.

Enrichment pipeline — AI auto-fills each recipe
Done

A cloud function that fires automatically whenever you swipe Yes on a recipe. It does four things in order: (1) scrapes the ingredients list from the recipe's source URL, (2) generates chef notes using Claude AI, (3) checks every ingredient against all 8 diet protocols, (4) looks up any uncertain ingredients against the FIG product database. If everything resolves cleanly it marks the recipe complete. If anything is uncertain it sends it to the Review Queue for you to decide manually.
